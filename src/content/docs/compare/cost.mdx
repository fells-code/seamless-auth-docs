The bill is often the first reason a team looks past its identity provider. This page puts a number
on it: enter your monthly active users (MAU), whether you sell to consumers (B2C) or businesses
(B2B), how many enterprise connections you need, and what kind of MFA, and it shows the published
list price of each Auth0, Okta Customer Identity, Amazon Cognito, Clerk, and Firebase
Authentication plan next to the cost of running Seamless Auth yourself.

These are **list prices only**, read from each vendor's own pricing page on the date given in
[Sources](#sources). They leave out discounts, annual commitments, negotiated contracts, taxes,
and usage charges a page does not print. Where a vendor publishes no price, the calculator says
"Contact sales" and links to the vendor. It never estimates one.

### Linking to a prefilled calculator

The calculator reads its inputs from the page address, so a link can open it already filled in.
For example, [`/compare/cost?mau=12000&vendor=cognito`](/compare/cost?mau=12000&vendor=cognito)
opens it at 12,000 MAU compared with Amazon Cognito.

| Parameter | Values |
| --- | --- |
| `mau` | A whole number from 1 to 1,000,000,000 |
| `vendor` | `all`, `auth0`, `okta`, `cognito`, `clerk`, or `firebase` |
| `useCase` | `b2c` or `b2b` |
| `enterpriseConnections` | A whole number from 0 to 10,000 |
| `mfa` | `none`, `otp`, or `enterprise` |

A missing or invalid value is ignored and that input keeps its default. The Seamless Auth row is
always shown.

## How The Numbers Are Read

**Auth0.** Auth0's pricing page has a MAU slider with steps at 500, 1,000, 2,500, 5,000, 7,500,
10,000, 20,000, and 30,000+, and separate B2C and B2B prices. The calculator uses the price the
page shows for each plan at each step, on monthly billing, and rounds your MAU up to the next step
(so 6,000 MAU uses the "up to 7,500" price). It does not interpolate between steps. Where a plan
shows "Get in touch" at a step, or your MAU is above 30,000, the calculator shows "Contact sales".
Free covers up to 25,000 MAU and has no paid path above that. Enterprise has no published price.

**Enterprise connections.** Auth0's Free plan includes 1 enterprise connection (plus unlimited
Okta enterprise connections), B2B Essentials includes 3, and B2B Professional includes 5. The B2C plans
list none on Essentials or Professional; the pricing page tells B2C customers to move to a B2B
plan for enterprise connections. On the B2B plans, extra connections are an add-on that the page
lists at "starting at $100 monthly per active connection", so any result that uses it is shown as
"From" the starting price. The page publishes no add-on for extra connections on Free.

**MFA.** The pricing page lists Pro MFA (one-time password apps and Duo) on every paid plan but not
on Free, and Enterprise MFA (SMS, voice, email, WebAuthn, and push through Auth0 Guardian) as
included on Professional, an add-on on B2B Essentials "starting at $100 monthly", and not listed on
B2C Essentials or Free. The page notes that SMS for MFA is charged separately by carriers. Where a
plan does not list the MFA you pick, the calculator says so rather than guessing a price.

**Okta Customer Identity.** Okta's own pricing page has a Customer Identity tab, separate from the
Auth0 pricing page it also links to. It lists an Enterprise base platform starting at $3,000 per
month, billed annually, with add-ons (including MFA and Inbound Federation) priced on usage such as
MAU and quoted by sales, and a B2C Suite and a B2B Suite with no published price. The calculator
shows the base platform as "From $3,000" whatever your inputs, because the page does not say how
MAU, MFA, or federation change it. See also
[Okta Customer Identity](/compare/auth0#okta-customer-identity) on the Auth0 comparison page.

**Amazon Cognito.** Cognito's pricing page has three user pool plans, Lite, Essentials, and Plus,
priced per monthly active user (MAU) for users who sign in directly or through a social provider.
Lite and Essentials include 10,000 MAU a month free per AWS account or organization; Plus has no
free tier. Above that, Essentials is $0.015 per MAU, Plus is $0.020, and Lite is tiered: $0.0055
from 10,001 to 100,000, $0.0046 to 1,000,000, $0.00325 to 10,000,000, and $0.0025 above, each rate
applying only to the users in its band. The page's tables have a region selector; the calculator
uses the US East (N. Virginia) rates. Users who sign in through SAML or OIDC federation are priced
separately on every plan: 50 MAU free, then $0.015 per MAU. The calculator takes a count of
enterprise connections, not federated users, so with any connection it shows the Cognito price as
"From" the direct sign-in price. SMS and email are billed through Amazon SNS and Amazon SES, and the
Cognito page prints no rate for them. The page also describes an older free tier of 50,000 MAU on
Lite for accounts that had user pools before 22 November 2024, the Advanced Security Features
add-on for Lite, machine-to-machine token charges, and multi-Region and higher-quota add-ons; the
calculator leaves these out. See also [Seamless Auth vs Amazon Cognito](/compare/cognito).

**Clerk.** Clerk bills monthly retained users (MRU), a user who returns at least a day after signing
up, counted per application, not MAU. The calculator uses your MAU as the MRU count, so for an app
with many one-time sign-ups it shows more than Clerk would charge. Hobby is free up to 50,000 MRU
per app and lists MFA and SSO connections as not included. Pro is $25 a month and Business $300 a
month on monthly billing ($20 and $250 billed annually); each includes 50,000 MRU per app, then
$0.02 per MRU to 100,000, $0.018 to 1,000,000, $0.015 to 10,000,000, and $0.012 above, each rate
applying only to the users in its band. Pro and Business include MFA (authenticator apps, SMS
codes, and backup codes) and 1 SSO connection (SAML, OIDC, or EASIE) per app; more connections are
$75 a month each up to 15 and $60 each up to 100, and the page says "Contact us" above that. SMS is
$0.01 per SMS in the US and Canada and market rate elsewhere. Clerk prices organizations for B2B
apps separately (100 monthly retained organizations included per app), and the calculator does not
count them. Enterprise has no published price. See also [Seamless Auth vs Clerk](/compare/clerk).

**Firebase Authentication.** Firebase's pricing page lists 50,000 MAU, and 50 SAML or OIDC MAU, at
no cost with Identity Platform on both the Spark (no cost) and Blaze (pay as you go) plans, and
links to Google Cloud pricing above that, which needs Blaze. The Identity Platform pricing page
lists email, phone, anonymous, and social users free up to 50,000 MAU, then $0.0055 per MAU to
100,000, $0.0046 to 1,000,000, $0.0032 to 10,000,000, and $0.0025 above, each rate applying only to
the users in its band. SAML and OIDC users are free up to 50 MAU and $0.015 per MAU after that, so,
as with Cognito, any enterprise connection makes the Firebase price a "From" price. Phone sign-in and
MFA are billed per SMS sent, $0.01 in the US and Canada and priced per country elsewhere, and the
first ten SMS a day are not billed. Firebase's pricing page also lists "Other Authentication
services" on both plans with no price; the calculator prices Firebase Authentication with Identity
Platform only. See also [Seamless Auth vs Firebase Authentication](/compare/firebase-auth).

## The Seamless Auth Number

Self-hosted Seamless Auth has no per-user, per-connection, or per-factor fee. You pay for the
infrastructure, so the cost is flat as MAU grows until you choose to add capacity. The calculator
uses one typical setup, priced from DigitalOcean's own pricing pages on 2026-10-21:

| | Basis | List price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Provider | DigitalOcean; the pricing pages list one price, not a price per region | |
| Auth API container | Basic Droplet, Regular CPU: 2 GiB memory, 1 vCPU, 50 GiB SSD, 2,000 GiB transfer | {usd(selfHost.app.monthlyUsd)} per month |
| Managed Postgres | Managed PostgreSQL, Standard Edition, Basic node: 1 GiB memory, 1 vCPU, single node, 10 GiB storage | {usd(selfHost.database.monthlyUsd)} per month |
| **Total** | | **{usd(selfHostMonthlyUsd)} per month** |

DigitalOcean's PostgreSQL pricing details page says single node clusters are not highly available
and recommends them for development and testing, and that high availability clusters begin at
$30.00 per month for the primary node plus at least one matching standby node at $30.00 per month.
The same page says that from 15 October 2026 for new accounts, and 30 November 2026 for all
accounts, new Standard Edition clusters cannot add standby nodes, so a new high availability
cluster needs a different edition. The single node plan the calculator uses is not affected.
Extra storage on a Standard Edition cluster costs $0.215 per GiB per month. That page gives
round starting prices (single node clusters from $15.00 per month); the calculator uses the
$15.15 that the Managed Databases pricing page lists for the plan.

This is a starting point, not a measured capacity. Seamless Auth has not published a benchmark of
how many users or sign-ins a given size handles. The one measurement on record is an idle API
container using about 73 MiB of memory with an empty database (see the
[Keycloak comparison's Footprint section](/compare/keycloak#footprint)). Size your deployment by
testing it with your own traffic.

The Seamless Auth number does **not** include:

- your time to deploy, upgrade, monitor, and secure it, which is the main cost of self-hosting
- backups beyond what your database provider includes, and their storage
- monitoring, logging, and alerting
- email and SMS delivery, which your providers (for example Twilio or AWS SNS) bill you for
- a load balancer, a second app instance, or a high availability database
- domain names and TLS certificates, if you pay for them

Seamless Auth also does less than these vendors. It has no SAML, no per-customer enterprise
connections, no SCIM, and no push or SMS second factor, so if your inputs need those, a lower price
does not mean the same product. The calculator notes this against the Seamless Auth row. See
[Seamless Auth vs Auth0](/compare/auth0) for the full comparison.

The managed Seamless Auth service is priced in the portal at
[dashboard.seamlessauth.com](https://dashboard.seamlessauth.com); this page does not quote it.
